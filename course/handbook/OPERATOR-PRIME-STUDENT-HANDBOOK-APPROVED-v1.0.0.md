## Who this is for, in one paragraph
Consenting adult engineers, doctoral-capable, with a background in non-destructive testing within materials engineering, all Toronto-based. No prior Kali or REA experience is assumed, but independent laboratory work is required. Every student has an M1 iPad Pro with LiDAR for room scanning and measurement against ground truth.

## The spine, every module, every lab, no exceptions
SEE → DOCUMENT → LOG → PREVENT. No prevention claim without that evidence chain. A claim without a log is just a story.

## Hard rules
- Controlled laboratory / own venues only. Own devices, own tags, own networks, own targets only, or targets explicitly authorised in writing.
- Consenting adults only. Face, voice, and biometric work: consenting adult participants, laboratory only, defensive education, templates and recordings deleted after the exercise, deletion log filed. Never family, never son, never strangers, never real-world surveillance or deployment.
- Radio transmit only on own authorised frequencies. Drone only in a safe, controlled flying area, Transport Canada checklist first. Lasers Class 1 only.
- Open-source hardware is the required standard for course targets, because a target that cannot be inspected cannot be honestly audited.
- Academic integrity: all evidence must be the student's own laboratory work, with sources, tools, versions, and commands recorded. Fabricated evidence is a failure of the laboratory, not a technical error.

## How a week works (template, no dates locked)
3 lab days, 1 evidence / homework day, 1 audit / inspection / lecture day, plus buffer days for catch-up only. Roles rotate daily: Operator (runs the test, records command and output), Recorder (owns the evidence log), Auditor (checks gates, inspects logs, verifies re-measurement). No student audits their own log alone.

## How a day works (in order, no clock times locked)
Opening brief (safety, consent, scope, hardware gate) → tool check (versions recorded) → lab setup (own target, isolated own network, ground truth) → morning test → midday review → afternoon test / re-test after hardening → evidence pack block → debrief (one finding per student, one inspection note, one correction) → close (tidy, targets secured, templates deleted where required, deletion log filed, next day's hardware gate checked). A day does not close with an unfiled log.

## Evidence, assessment, and gates
Assessment: evidence pack 40%, written report (GenOffice) 25%, narrated summary (VoiceStudio) 15%, defence brief and capstone defence 20%. Labs are completed in order, one at a time. A laboratory is not complete until its evidence is filed. A failed gate is resubmitted until it passes. No mark is awarded for a fabricated, incomplete, or unverifiable result. Capstone (L20, and L50 in the year draft) is scored on evidence quality first, then completeness, then clarity. Speed alone does not win.

## What to bring, what to file, what never to do
Bring: your own devices and targets, your consent records, your measurement tools. File: tool, version, command, output, measurement, error, and file path for every step. Never: test a target you do not own or are not authorised for, record a non-consenting person, keep a template after its exercise, or reproduce an attack prompt in a log. Defence commentary only.

## If something goes wrong
Stop the test. Log the method and the error honestly. Tell the professor and the Dean through the log, not by hiding the failure. A logged failure with a resubmission plan is good laboratory work. A hidden failure is not.
