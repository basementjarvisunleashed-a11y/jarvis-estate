## 3. Breathing room, audits, inspections, and a stretched Olympics (DRAFT)

Last time: 3 buffer days for 82 teaching days, no spare teaching week.
Year-long, that ratio is NOT carried forward. Draft structure, NOT final:

- **Buffer days: at least 1 buffer / catch-up day per teaching week, plus
  1 buffer week per term / block.** In a year-long, Monday–Friday framework,
  that is roughly 1 day in 5 for catch-up, evidence completion, and re-tests,
  instead of 3 days in 82. Exact count is NOT locked until the year and the
  holiday calendar are locked (Section 4) — the principle is locked: buffer is
  a regular rhythm, not a last-minute rescue.
- **Audits: monthly, plus quarterly, plus final.** Monthly audit = evidence
  packs, logs, and hardening answers for that month's labs, scored on evidence
  quality, not speed. Quarterly audit = full estate audit (tools, hardware,
  models, workspace trust, licences), with a corrections ledger. Final audit =
  full year evidence review before L50.
- **Inspections: weekly lab inspections + monthly venue inspections.**
  Weekly: lab safety, consent logs, device / tag inventory, evidence folders.
  Monthly: airfield and farm venue checks (drone rules, radio rules, night
  safety, weather gates), hardware inventory (H1–H30), and model / workspace
  trust checks (no external AGENTS file auto-loaded, no unreviewed Modelfile).
- **Hectic, nice, and epic — by design, not by overload:** a typical week is
  3 lab days + 1 evidence / homework day + 1 audit / inspection / lecture day.
  Hectic means full, with evidence every week, not rushed with no evidence.
  Epic means the year builds: foundation → spectrum / cards / chips → mesh /
  vision / brain → aerial / night / space → AI defense → supply chain /
  incident response → final defence. Each block ends with an audit, not just
  a next lab.
- **Final Olympics, stretched:** L50 is NOT a single week. Draft stretch:
  Week 1 — rehearsal and evidence review. Week 2 — team challenges, scored on
  evidence quality, not speed alone. Week 3 — defence briefs and capstone
  defence, verified by re-measurement. Buffer days sit inside each week, so a
  failed gate can be resubmitted without breaking the final. L20 Olympics
  remains as the mid-course version, shorter, in the same scoring spirit.

## 5. Homework, lecture notes, Graphify, Image, Cartographer — sectioned out (DRAFT)

Every module, M0–M14 and every future module, gets the same sectioned
structure. No module is taught without all five sections below, in draft.

- **Lecture notes (per module):** objectives, concepts from zero, worked
  example, safety / consent brief, evidence standard, and a closing check
  (what a student must be able to show, not just say). Written in GenOffice
  (Stack 10, local), narrated in VoiceStudio (Stack 9, local), professional
  register for course materials (no sports / entertainment references, per
  HgX's course rule).
- **Homework assignments (per module):** reading + REA teardown write-up +
  evidence log + one hardening answer. Homework is NOT a quiz alone — it is
  a small evidence pack: what was seen, what was documented, what was logged,
  what would prevent it. Failed homework is resubmitted until it passes,
  same as labs. Homework never asks a student to run a tool against a real
  person, system, or network, or to reproduce an attack prompt.
- **Graphify (Stack 3, local, knowledge graph):** every module builds a
  knowledge graph — tools, attacks, defenses, evidence, hardware, and their
  relationships — in Graphify, local only, Cloud excluded, semantic pass OFF
  unless a local backend is used. The graph is part of the evidence pack:
  a student who cannot graph the relationships has not yet understood the
  module. Graphify is brought in as the course's memory and mapping tool,
  not as a new software stack (stacks are HgX's).
- **Image (ComfyUI + HyperFrames, local):** lecture visuals, lab diagrams,
  evidence visuals, and narrated lecture machines, built locally (ComfyUI
  Stack 5, HyperFrames Stack 4, hosted excluded). Images are teaching aids,
  not evidence by themselves — evidence is still logs, measurements, and
  re-measurement. No image is presented as a result without its log.
- **Cartographer (mapping track, NOT a new software stack):** the mapping
  thread across the course — LiDAR maps (H17–H19 + iPad), drone maps
  (airfield / farm), satellite maps (SatNOGS), mesh maps (watch / T-Beam /
  T-Deck / XIAO), and farm / airfield site maps. Cartographer is the role
  and the track: a student who can map a site, with ground truth, and defend
  the map with evidence, has completed the cartography thread. Mapping tools
  are the existing stacks and hardware; no Cartographer software stack is
  added by this file, per HgX's "I'll worry about the software stack".
