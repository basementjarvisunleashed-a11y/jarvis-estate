## How to contribute, in order
1. Propose first: state what you propose to add or correct, which file and version it touches, and what evidence, log, or capture supports it, in writing, to the Dean.
2. Work openly: work in a versioned draft, with a changelog naming what changed, never by overwriting a locked file, never by deleting anything.
3. Credit honestly: state your own contribution, credit every source you used in full (HGX Engineering, HGX Academy, and Jarvis for course material, plus every third-party source in the Third-Party Attribution Register), and do not claim credit for work that is not yours.
4. Scrub it: no personal data, no sealed substance, no prompt text, no non-owned target, no non-consenting person, in any contribution.
5. Dean review: the Dean reviews the contribution against the canon, the licences, and the guardrails, and files it as a new version, or returns it with a named correction, or declines it with a filed reason. A contribution is not part of the course until the Dean files it and HgX approves the exact content for publication.

## Ownership of contributions (template, final text to be locked on legal review)
A contribution that is filed as part of the course is published under the course licences (MIT for code, CC-BY-4.0 for text), with the contributor credited in the changelog and in the file's credit section, unless a separate, filed agreement states otherwise. No contribution transfers ownership of the original course, and no contributor acquires ownership of the course by contributing, unless a separate, filed agreement states otherwise. If HgX orders a contributor agreement, it will be filed as its own locked version, and this guide will be corrected as a new version naming it.
