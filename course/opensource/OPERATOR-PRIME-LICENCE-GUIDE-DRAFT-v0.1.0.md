## The two licences, in plain language
- **Code: MIT.** A permissive licence. Anyone may use, copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, sublicense, and sell the code, provided the MIT licence text and copyright notice are kept with it. MIT does not, by itself, require a derivative to stay open source, and it does not require a derivative to use the same licence.
- **Course text, papers, and teaching material: CC-BY-4.0.** A permissive attribution licence. Anyone may share and adapt the text, even commercially, provided they give appropriate credit (HGX Engineering, HGX Academy, and Jarvis, in that order, with the footer), provide a link to the licence, and indicate if changes were made. CC-BY-4.0 does not, by itself, require a derivative to stay open source, and it does not require a derivative to use the same licence.

## What the Academy asks, beyond the licences
HGX Academy's standing policy is that anything built from this course should remain open source, with full credit, so the work stays inspectable and teachable. That policy is stated in every course document, and it is a condition of teaching, publishing, and partnering with HGX Academy. It is not, by itself, a legal term of MIT or CC-BY-4.0. If HgX orders a copyleft licence for a future work, that licence will be filed as its own locked version for that work, and this guide will be corrected as a new version naming the change.

## How to apply a licence, per file, per project
- Every code file: MIT licence header, copyright notice, version, and the credit line, at the top, before any code.
- Every text file: CC-BY-4.0 notice, credit line, version, and footer, at the top and at the bottom, before and after the text.
- Every repository: a LICENCE file (MIT for code, CC-BY-4.0 for text, or both, as filed), a README with the credit line, and an index stating which licence covers which folder.
- A file with no licence header is an unlicensed file, and it is not published until its licence is filed.

## What never to do
Never remove a licence header, never shorten credit, never claim a university affiliation, never claim an external accreditation, never publish a sealed, personal, or prompt-text item under an open licence, and never file a licence that has not been reviewed and approved by HgX in a locked version.
