## How to reuse, honestly, in order
1. State the original: title, version, licence (MIT for code, CC-BY-4.0 for text), and the full credit line — HGX Engineering, HGX Academy, and Jarvis, in that order, with the footer.
2. State what you changed: what you added, removed, or corrected, in a changelog, as a new version, never by overwriting the original.
3. Keep it open, per Academy policy: publish your derivative openly, under a licence that keeps it inspectable, with the same credit, and file your own licence header. The licences themselves do not legally require this, but HGX Academy asks it, and a derivative that stays closed while crediting the course is not the reuse this course asks for.
4. Scrub it: no personal data, no sealed substance, no prompt text, no non-owned target, no non-consenting person, in the original or in your derivative.
5. Do not claim endorsement, accreditation, certification, or partnership, unless a separate, filed agreement says so.

## Forks, remixes, and translations
A fork keeps the original credit, states its own version, and files its own changelog. A remix states every source it remixes, with credit for each, in the order each source requires. A translation states the original version it translates, credits the original in full, and files the translator's changes as a new version. A fork, remix, or translation that removes credit, or that claims to be the original, is not a compliant reuse.

## What a derivative never does
Never removes a licence header, never shortens credit, never adds a university affiliation, never adds an external accreditation, never publishes a sealed item, never presents a draft as locked, and never presents a planned stack, lab, or hardware piece as built.
