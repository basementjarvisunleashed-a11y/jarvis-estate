## Open source from day one, rock bottom
Jarvis is open sourced from day one. Everything about OPERATOR PRIME is aimed toward open-sourcing it: the course, all the open-source hardware used in the course, and the bracelet — all open source, as 1.0. The course is embedded in 1.0, not a separate pipeline and not a side project. HGX Academy is the 1.0 course vehicle.
The system goes public. HgX's private life never does: family, finances, credentials, personal data, live location, and sealed substance are scrubbed out of every public output, always.

## Should remain open source — what that means, honestly
- Legal licences: code under MIT, course text, papers, and teaching material under CC-BY-4.0 (see the Licence Guide). Those licences are permissive: they require credit, and they permit reuse, remixing, and redistribution, including in a closed work, unless a different licence is filed for that work.
- Academy policy: HGX Academy's standing policy, and HgX's stated intent in this manifesto, is that anything built from this course should remain open source, with full credit, so the work stays inspectable, auditable, and teachable. That policy is a condition of teaching, publishing, and partnering with HGX Academy, and it is stated in every course document — but it is not, by itself, a legal term of MIT or CC-BY-4.0, and this manifesto does not pretend otherwise.
- If HgX orders a copyleft licence for a future work (a licence that legally requires derivatives to stay open), that licence will be filed as its own locked version for that work, and this manifesto will be corrected as a new version naming the change. Until then, no file here claims a copyleft term that has not been filed.

## Why open, in one paragraph
A target that cannot be inspected cannot be honestly audited. A course that cannot be read cannot be honestly taught. Open source is not a marketing line here — it is the evidence standard: see it, document it, log it, prevent it, with the log filed and the licence filed beside it.
