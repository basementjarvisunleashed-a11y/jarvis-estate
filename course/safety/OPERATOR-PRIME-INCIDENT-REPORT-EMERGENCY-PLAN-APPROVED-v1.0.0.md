## Emergency plan, every day, every venue
- Before any test: safety brief given, venue boundaries stated, nearest hospital / clinic and assembly point stated and filed, first-aid kit checked, phone charged, emergency contacts for the day filed (blanks, per cohort).
- On any injury, fire, electrical fault, drone loss of control, or safety failure: stop all tests, make the area safe, call 911 where required, give first aid within training, do not move a seriously injured person except from immediate danger, log the time-order and every action taken.
- After: incident report filed the same day (see below), professor tells the Dean through the log, risk assessment reviewed before the affected lab runs again, insurance record checked where applicable.

## Incident report form (copy per incident, file the same day)
- Lab code, venue type, date-order, time-order, persons involved (roles only, no personal data beyond what the report strictly needs, and no personal data in any published copy).
- What happened, in order, with method and error, as honestly as the lab log requires.
- What stopped, what was made safe, what emergency action was taken, who was told, and when.
- Injury / damage / loss, stated plainly, or "none" where none occurred — never "none" by assumption where an injury or damage is unknown.
- Root cause, correction, re-test plan, and the gate that must pass before the lab runs again.
- Dean review: signed, filed as a new version, never overwriting the original report.

## Near-miss rule
A near-miss is filed exactly like an incident, minus the injury section. A near-miss that is not filed is a failed safety log, and the next lab day does not start until it is filed.
