## 6. How a week and a day work
Week template, no dates locked: 3 lab days, 1 evidence / homework day, 1 audit / inspection / lecture day, plus buffer days for catch-up only. Roles rotate daily: Operator, Recorder, Auditor. No student audits their own log alone.
Day, in order, no clock times locked: opening brief (safety, consent, scope, hardware gate) → tool check (versions recorded) → lab setup (own target, isolated own network, ground truth) → morning test → midday review → afternoon test / re-test after hardening → evidence pack block → debrief (one finding per student, one inspection note, one correction) → close (tidy, targets secured, templates deleted where required, deletion log filed, next day's hardware gate checked). A day does not close with an unfiled log.

## 8. Evidence, assessment, gates, and progression
Assessment: evidence pack 40%, written report (GenOffice) 25%, narrated summary (VoiceStudio) 15%, defence brief and capstone defence 20%. Labs completed in order, one at a time. A laboratory is not complete until its evidence is filed. A failed gate is resubmitted until it passes. No mark for fabricated, incomplete, or unverifiable results. Capstone scored on evidence quality first, then completeness, then clarity. Speed alone does not win.
Evidence pack standard, per laboratory: checklist, tool log (tool, version, command, output, measurement, error, file path, in order), captures, analysis (confidence scored BEFORE the write-up, one ledger row per finding, hardening change, before-and-after test results), report draft, narrated summary draft, defence brief draft, logs (daily, deletion where required, inspection, corrections), Graphify update (flaw, device, method, defence linked, local only).
Progression: next lab only when the current lab's gate has passed and its pack is filed; next module only when every lab in the current module has passed; capstone only when every lab before it has passed. Missed days are caught up on buffer days, with a filed plan. No new teardown on a buffer homework day — catch-up only.

## 10. Conduct, complaints, appeals, data, and the rest of a real course
Conduct: honest logs only, own targets only, consent only, no personal data in any course material, no altering another student's log, no deleting or overwriting anything. Complaints: in writing, logged the same day, Dean review, filed decision. Gate / mark appeal: in writing, with the pack attached, Dean review, filed decision, final appeal to HgX, founder, HGX Academy. Data: minimisation everywhere, scrubbing before any external sharing, templates and recordings deleted after the exercise, deletion log filed. Accessibility: written and narrated materials, plain language, glossary for every term, Dean-approved equivalent labs with their own gates where a standard lab cannot be completed accessibly, observance / illness catch-up on buffer days, never penalised for a filed observance or illness caught up on a buffer day.
If something goes wrong: stop the test, log the method and the error honestly, tell the professor and the Dean through the log, not by hiding the failure. A logged failure with a resubmission plan is good laboratory work. A hidden failure is not.
