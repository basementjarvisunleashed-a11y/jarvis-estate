## The PhD-level standard in this guide

A claim in this course is doctoral-standard only when it meets all five tests, and the professor must hold every one of them, every week:

1. **Defined:** every load-bearing term is defined exactly as taught, with its source stated.
2. **Reproducible:** the method is written so another operator, on the same own devices and own targets, can repeat it and get the same measurement, or a stated reason why not.
3. **Falsifiable:** the claim states what measurement would disprove it.
4. **Audited:** the evidence pack, the gate, and any re-measurement are filed, indexed, and versioned — never overwritten, never deleted.
5. **Defended by re-measurement:** a challenged claim is answered with a new measurement, not with a louder restatement.

If a student's work fails any test, the professor marks that failure plainly, requires re-measurement, and records the gap. That is not harshness; it is the course.

## How to teach this course, in one page

1. **Read before you teach.** Read the module guide, the weekly professor guide, the student worksheet, and the lab report template in full before the week begins. Never teach from memory, never teach from a summary of a summary.
2. **Quote load-bearing definitions exactly.** Where a definition carries the lab, give it word for word as taught, and state its source. Paraphrase everything else freely, but never paraphrase a definition into a different claim.
3. **Lab first, lecture second.** Students learn this course by running the lab on own devices and own targets, in the controlled lab — not by watching the professor run it.
4. **Gate on evidence.** No lab, checkpoint, module, or capstone passes on assertion, on a good presentation, or on effort alone. It passes when the measurement is filed and, where challenged, re-measured.
5. **Teach failure as material.** A documented failure with a re-measurement is worth more than a smooth success with no evidence. Say so, mark so, and mean it.
6. **Homework is writing, not repetition.** The homework day exists so the student writes the lab in their own words, names the strongest and weakest evidence, and asks one real question for audit day.
7. **Audit day is teaching, not administration.** The inspection, the evidence review, and the open-thread carry-forward are where the professor sees what actually landed.

## Daily, weekly, monthly rhythm (professor side)

- **Daily (lab days):** prep devices and own targets the day before; run the pre-lab safety and consent checklist; teach the method, then step back; file the day's evidence before anyone leaves.
- **Daily (homework day):** set the homework from the weekly student pack, read it for evidence quality (not for agreement with the professor), and return one precise feedback note per student.
- **Daily (audit day):** run the weekly lab inspection, review every evidence pack against the gate, and carry every open thread forward in writing.
- **Weekly:** follow the Weekly Professor Guide for that week (52 guides, W01–W52); report the week's gates, failures, and open threads to the Dean feed.
- **Monthly:** follow the Monthly Professor Plan and the Monthly Marking & Feedback Guide; run the monthly audit; inspect every venue used; review the month's homework as a body of work, not one by one in isolation.
- **Quarterly:** run the Quarterly Professor Review; review all checkpoints touched, all examinations marked, and Project 25 progress; set remediation, not just grades.
- **Year:** run the Final Year Professor Review; account for every lab, every examination, and every open gap; file the year rollup and the scrubbed learning summary to HgX alone, via the Dean.

## Assessment — how the 40 / 25 / 15 / 20 split is taught and marked

- **40 — Evidence pack:** completeness, reproducibility, filed measurements, re-measurements where challenged. A missing measurement is a missing mark, however good the writing.
- **25 — Report:** method written so it can be repeated, limits stated honestly, failures documented.
- **15 — Narration:** the student explains the work in their own words, live or written, and answers a challenge with evidence, not with confidence.
- **20 — Defence brief:** a short brief defended against a sceptical auditor, verified by re-measurement where the auditor challenges it.
- **No pass mark is stated in this draft.** Progression decisions are the professor's, on marking, with Dean review — never pre-filled, never assumed from attendance.
