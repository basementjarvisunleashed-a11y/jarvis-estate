## The PhD-level standard in this guide

A claim in this course is doctoral-standard only when it meets all five tests, and the professor must hold every one of them, every week:

1. **Defined:** every load-bearing term is defined exactly as taught, with its source stated.
2. **Reproducible:** the method is written so another operator, on the same own devices and own targets, can repeat it and get the same measurement, or a stated reason why not.
3. **Falsifiable:** the claim states what measurement would disprove it.
4. **Audited:** the evidence pack, the gate, and any re-measurement are filed, indexed, and versioned — never overwritten, never deleted.
5. **Defended by re-measurement:** a challenged claim is answered with a new measurement, not with a louder restatement.

If a student's work fails any test, the professor marks that failure plainly, requires re-measurement, and records the gap. That is not harshness; it is the course.

## Monthly plan — what the professor does, in order

1. **Before the month:** read all four (or, for Month 12, eight) weekly professor guides, all lab sheets, and last month's audit and marking review. Prepare devices, targets, and venue bookings for the whole month, not week by week.
2. **Progression:** teach the month as a progression, not as isolated labs — name, at the month's opening, what the month as a whole will prove, and what it will leave open for later months.
3. **Checkpoints:** review every checkpoint touched this month against evidence, and record which checkpoints are passed, which need re-measurement, and which are carried forward.
4. **Project 25:** review the month's contribution to Project 25 prove-out against its workbook, and set the next prove-out step in writing.
5. **Venues:** inspect every venue used this month (lab weekly, venues monthly); file the inspection before the month's audit.
6. **Students at risk:** list, privately, any student whose evidence quality is falling, and set remediation on buffer days — never by lowering the gate.
