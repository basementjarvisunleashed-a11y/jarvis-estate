## The PhD-level standard in this guide

A claim in this course is doctoral-standard only when it meets all five tests, and the professor must hold every one of them, every week:

1. **Defined:** every load-bearing term is defined exactly as taught, with its source stated.
2. **Reproducible:** the method is written so another operator, on the same own devices and own targets, can repeat it and get the same measurement, or a stated reason why not.
3. **Falsifiable:** the claim states what measurement would disprove it.
4. **Audited:** the evidence pack, the gate, and any re-measurement are filed, indexed, and versioned — never overwritten, never deleted.
5. **Defended by re-measurement:** a challenged claim is answered with a new measurement, not with a louder restatement.

If a student's work fails any test, the professor marks that failure plainly, requires re-measurement, and records the gap. That is not harshness; it is the course.

## Month 10 marking (professor side, criteria only)

- **Weeks covered:** 37–40. **No exam key and no model answer is in this guide** — it is published open source, so anything in it is visible to students too. Mark from the criteria below, applied to each student's own work.

## Criteria — the 40 / 25 / 15 / 20 split, applied to the month's work

- **40 — Evidence pack:** Are all measurements filed, with method, target, and limits? Were challenged claims re-measured? Is the pack indexed and versioned?
- **25 — Report:** Can the method be repeated from the writing alone? Are limits and failures stated honestly?
- **15 — Narration:** Does the student explain the work in their own words and answer a challenge with evidence, not with confidence?
- **20 — Defence brief:** Does the brief defend a conclusion against a sceptical auditor, verified by re-measurement where challenged?

## Feedback — how to write it

Every marked piece gets three lines, no more, no fewer: (1) one precise strength, with the evidence that shows it; (2) one precise gap, with the evidence that shows it; (3) the exact re-measurement that would close the gap. Vague praise ("good work") and vague criticism ("needs improvement") are both marking failures — the professor's, not the student's.
