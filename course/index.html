What you will be able to do — seven outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to do these seven things. Each one is proven with filed evidence, not claimed.

  1. Build and measure a laboratory estate — with ground truth, and quantify your own error.
  2. Sense spectrum, cards, Bluetooth, chips, mesh, vision, and aerial systems — with open-source hardware, and prove what you sensed with a filed log.
  3. Reverse engineer your own firmware and applications, locally, with REA — and turn a verified finding into a teachable, fully documented presentation.
  4. Harden what you broke, re-test it, and file before-and-after results that prove the defence.
  5. Build a local knowledge graph of your own findings — and answer a stated question only from filed laboratory findings.
  6. Audit an entire laboratory estate in one day — file findings per area, and write a defence brief.
  7. Present a capstone defence, verified by re-measurement — with a full evidence pack, report, narration, and defence brief.

How the course runs — the week, the day, the path

A proper academy runs on a rhythm you can set your watch by. Here is ours.

📅 The week

Three lab days, one homework day, one audit day, plus buffers. The Dean feeds the Professor daily, weekly, and monthly, so every week arrives planned — that day's lab and module, the hardware gate, the opening brief, the tool check, the lab steps, the debrief prompt, and the close checklist.

🗺️ The path

Modules M0 to M14, in order, one at a time. Each module carries its laboratories. Each laboratory ends at a gate. Twenty-one checkpoints mark your progress. Fifty laboratories (L1–L50) locked for 1.0. L20 Operator Olympics is the mid-course capstone; L50 Year Capstone Final Defence (three weeks) ends the course; then Project 25 proves the entire suite.

🚪 What a gate is

A gate is the check at the end of a laboratory. You file your evidence pack — checklist, raw outputs, measurements, logs — and the gate passes or it does not. A failed gate is resubmitted until it passes. No gate, no progress.

📍 What a checkpoint is

A checkpoint is a marked point on the path — 21 of them across the course — where your progress is reviewed against the evidence you have filed. Checkpoints are how the academy knows where you stand.

📁 What an evidence pack is

One folder per laboratory: checklist, raw outputs, measurements, and logs. Your evidence pack is 40% of your mark, and it is the foundation of everything else — no evidence, no report, no narration, no defence.

🏁 What Project 25 is

Project 25 proves the entire suite — the whole course, the whole stack, the whole lab, end to end, with no faked passes. It runs right after the build, before other projects. It is the final proof, not another lab.

Roles rotate: Operator, Recorder, Auditor — so you learn to do the work, record the work, and check the work. Teaching schedule and year logs are in the files table below (draft v0.1.0).

Assessment — evidence-based, no exceptions

No laboratory passes on assertion alone. A failed gate is resubmitted until it passes. No mark is awarded for a fabricated, incomplete, or unverifiable result.

40%
Evidence pack — one folder per laboratory, with checklist, raw outputs, measurements, and logs
25%
Written report (GenOffice) — findings, method, error analysis, and defence, with tracked revisions
15%
Narrated summary (VoiceStudio) — clear, professional narration of method and result
20%
Defence brief and capstone defence — presentation of the capstone, including a defence verified by re-measurement

Project 25: prove the entire suite. Twenty-one family checkpoints (fifteen core plus GPU, Bluetooth, RFID, SDR, VoIP, and Labs). Each checkpoint requires tool presence, a safe self-test, and saved evidence.
